# The Psychology of People Who Want to Do Everything

## Summary

A 15-minute video essay (Deep Primate) that reframes "I'm interested in everything" as a structural problem rather than a discipline problem. The opening premise: maybe you don't lack discipline — maybe you have too many things you want to become. You start the day wanting one career, see a sport and wonder about being an athlete, hear a song and want to learn an instrument, discover filmmaking or business or psychology, and each new interest feels like evidence you chose wrong.

The core argument is that choosing one path feels like killing the other versions of yourself, so people avoid committing and stay permanently in the exploration phase. The video's answer is not "pick one and ignore the rest":

- **Breadth vs depth** — breadth is not the enemy; unfocused breadth is. The distinction is whether the interests compound into something or just accumulate.
- **The M-shaped career** — instead of one deep specialisation (T-shape), build several areas of genuine expertise over time, with the combination itself being the differentiator. Multiple peaks, not one peak plus shallow dabbling.
- **You don't have to choose your whole life today** — the decision in front of you is the next commitment, not the identity you'll have at 50.
- **Strategic quitting** — leave things deliberately when they stop paying compound interest, instead of drifting or quitting by guilt.
- **Externalise the ideas** — capture interests and projects somewhere so the mind stops holding them all open at once; the overwhelm is largely a working-memory problem.

## Key Takeaways

- The pain of "too many interests" comes from treating every interest as a competing identity rather than as a possible input to one combined skill set.
- Sequence beats selection: commit to one thing for a defined period, keep the others parked in writing, revisit on a schedule.
- Strategic quitting needs a criterion (does this still compound?) — otherwise it becomes avoidance.
- A written capture system is part of the method, not a productivity side-quest; it is what makes parking an interest possible.

## My Notes

- This maps almost exactly onto the last few years: graphic/UI design → call-centre and network support → structured cabling → help desk, with security certifications and AI tooling on the side. That is not indecision, it is an M being built in the wrong order — the combination (networks + security + design + automation) is genuinely rare and is the thing to lean into.
- The practical version for me: two anchor peaks (network/infrastructure + AI-agent automation), with design as the supporting skill that makes the output of the other two presentable. Everything else (3D, motion, music, hardware tinkering) is allowed as a parked interest, not a fourth career.
- "Strategic quitting" is the uncomfortable one: stop collecting certificates and side projects that don't compound into the two anchors.
- The capture step is already solved — this vault *is* the parking lot. The missing piece is a scheduled review of the parked items rather than one more inbox.
- Worth rewatching before the next big commitment decision (job move, specialisation, or a new side project).
