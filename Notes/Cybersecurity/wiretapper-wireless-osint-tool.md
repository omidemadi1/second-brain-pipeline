## Summary

WireTapper is a wireless OSINT tool that uses passive signal intelligence to discover and map radio-based devices without active scanning. It detects and correlates signals from Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, CCTV cameras, and vehicles to reveal the invisible wireless landscape. The tool operates passively — meaning it doesn't transmit or actively probe, making it stealthy and legally safer in many jurisdictions. Shared by Tom Dörr (@tom_doerr), who curates GitHub repos, DSPy, and AI agent content.

## Key Takeaways

- **Passive operation**: No active scanning/transmission — listens only, reducing detection risk
- **Multi-protocol coverage**: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, CCTV, and vehicle signals in one tool
- **Correlation engine**: Links signals across protocols to build a unified device map
- **OSINT focus**: Designed for intelligence gathering, not exploitation
- **Open source**: Available on GitHub (h9zdev/WireTapper) for inspection and contribution

## My Notes

- Passive signal intelligence is a growing niche — useful for physical security assessments, red teaming, and privacy audits
- Vehicle signal detection likely uses TPMS (tire pressure monitoring) and key fob signals
- CCTV detection may leverage wireless camera protocols or RF leakage
- Could integrate with existing OSINT frameworks (Maltego, SpiderFoot) for enriched profiles
- Legal considerations vary by jurisdiction — passive listening is generally more permissible than active scanning
